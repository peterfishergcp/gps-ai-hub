# Citation Guide

Cite the governing law as the law writes it. The provisions in this guide are
sourced (see Sources) and may be cited when they cover the item. For any other
provision, cite it only if it appears in an agency policy or record the user
provided; otherwise write the act's name and section with "provision to confirm"
and add a flag. An invented citation fails the officer's review, while
"provision to confirm" costs the officer one lookup.

## Federal Records

Content                                             | Cite
--------------------------------------------------- | ----
Personal information                                | Exemption 6, `5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(6)`
Personal information in a law enforcement record    | `5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(6), (b)(7)(C)`
Health information                                  | Exemption 6 (HIPAA is not an exemption)
FTI                                                 | Exemption 3 with 26 U.S.C. § 6103, for example `5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(3); 26 U.S.C. § 6103`
Record made confidential by another federal statute | Exemption 3 with the statute

Grounds outside the default set: name the likely exemption in the officer flag
and do not redact.

Ground                                                                          | Likely exemption to name in the flag
------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------
Predecisional recommendations, legal advice, attorney-client privilege          | Exemption 5, 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(5); withholding also requires foreseeable harm (5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(8))
Active investigation                                                            | Exemption 7(A), 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(A)
Confidential source, or a witness who asks not to be named or fears retaliation | Exemption 7(D), 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(D), if confidentiality was promised or implied
Law enforcement techniques, security details                                    | Exemption 7(E), 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(E)
Names of law enforcement personnel                                              | Exemptions 6 and 7(C); say you do not know the agency's practice if you don't

For a federal agency's incident, patient safety, or quality review report, flag
whether a federal statute may make the whole report confidential through
Exemption 3, 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(3). Name a statute only if you know one applies
to this agency (for example, 38 U.S.C. § 5705 covers only VA medical
quality-assurance records); otherwise write "statute to confirm". Say that you
don't have enough information to decide whether it covers the report. Do not
withhold the report on that basis; redact the default set as usual.

Governing law line for a federal record: `Federal FOIA, 5 U.S.C. § 552`, adding
the agency's FOIA regulations when you know them (for example, EPA's FOIA
regulations, 40 CFR Part 2; DHS's FOIA regulations, 6 CFR Part 5).

Immigration status information (asylum, credible fear, protected status): redact
the person's identifiers and statements under Exemptions 6 and 7(C), and flag
the status information for the officer; cite no specific regulation for it.

## State and Local Records

-   Cite the state act's own provision when you know it, for example `C.R.S.
    24-72-204(3)(a)(I)` for medical data under the Colorado Open Records Act.
-   When you know the act but not the provision, write the act's section and
    "provision to confirm", for example `C.R.S. 24-72-204, provision to
    confirm`, and add a flag. In the log, write `{Act name}; provision to
    confirm`.
-   Some states use a balancing test rather than a list of exemptions (for
    example Arizona). Cite the law and the recognized exception, for example
    `A.R.S. § 39-121, privacy` or `A.R.S. § 39-121, best interests of the
    state`, and add a flag asking the officer to confirm and record the
    balancing.
-   **FTI on a state record**: cite the state act's provision for records that
    federal law makes confidential, with 26 U.S.C. § 6103, for example `C.R.S.
    24-72-204(1)(b); 26 U.S.C. § 6103`. Flag that agencies receiving FTI usually
    have an incident process when FTI appears in an ordinary email.
-   **Other federal confidentiality statutes on a state record** (FERPA, 42 CFR
    Part 2, 28 U.S.C. § 534): cite them through the state act's provision for
    records made confidential by federal law, never on their own, for example
    `C.R.S. 24-72-204(1)(b); 20 U.S.C. § 1232g`. If you don't know that
    provision, write "provision to confirm".
-   **Health information on a state record**: cite the state act's medical or
    privacy provision, or "provision to confirm". HIPAA is not a public records
    exemption. If the user asks you to cite HIPAA, cite the state provision
    instead and flag the declined request.
-   **Privilege or deliberative material on a state record**: flag it with the
    state act's privilege or deliberative provision if you know it, or
    "provision to confirm". Do not redact it.

Governing law line for a state or local record: `{Act name} ({citation} and
following), {type of agency}`, for example `Colorado Open Records Act (C.R.S.
24-72-200.1 and following), a state agency` or `Arizona public records law
(A.R.S. § 39-121 and following), a city`.

## Federal Statutes That Make a Whole Record Confidential

When one of these covers the record, propose withholding the whole record in a
flag (never silently) and still redact the identifiers in the draft in case any
part is released:

-   Substance use disorder treatment records: 42 U.S.C. § 290dd-2; 42 CFR
    Part 2.
-   Student education records: FERPA, 20 U.S.C. § 1232g. FERPA applies only if
    the record is an education record; say so in the flag.
-   Criminal history from the FBI or NCIC: 28 U.S.C. § 534; 28 CFR 20.33.
-   FTI, including child support and tax refund offset information: 26 U.S.C. §
    6103(l)(6) and (l)(10).

In a child support or offset record, FTI is what came from the IRS: the refund
or offset amount, the return details (joint return, spouse, injured spouse
claim), and any figure that reveals them by arithmetic (for example, a remaining
balance that equals certified arrears minus the offset). Redact those, and flag
that the officer confirms the scope. The agency's own figures that did not come
from the IRS (certified arrears, state-reported sales or tax figures, the case
number, program procedures) are not FTI and stay, with a flag asking the officer
whether Exemption 6 or a state privacy or tax confidentiality provision should
also cover them.

An applicant-supplied tax return (for example, a Form 1040 in a grant file) is
not FTI unless it came from the IRS. Redact its identifiers and propose
withholding the return in a flag.

When a state's own law may make a record type confidential as a whole (for
example, a child welfare report), handle it the same way: propose withholding in
a flag, say you don't have enough information on the state provision, and redact
the identifiers in the draft. Sourced state provision: in Arizona, information
the Department of Child Safety (DCS) gathers about a report or investigation of
child abuse or neglect is confidential under A.R.S. § 8-807 (DCS information;
public disclosure; confidentiality); name it in the withholding flag.

## Jargon

-   **Redaction**: removing content from the released copy, not hiding it
    visually.
-   **PII / PHI / FTI**: personally identifiable information; protected health
    information; federal tax information.
-   **Exemption 6 / 7(C)**: the federal FOIA privacy exemptions, 5 U.S.C. §
    552(b)(6) and (b)(7)(C).
-   **Segregable**: the parts of a record that can be released once exempt
    content is removed.
